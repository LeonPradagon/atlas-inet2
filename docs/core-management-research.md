# Fiber core management: primary-source research

Research date: 2026-11-23. Scope: physical inventory, paths, splices, PON splitters and capacity reservation. Here **core** means one individually identifiable optical fiber in a cable, not an optical fiber's glass core. The recommendations below distinguish source facts from proposed inventory rules; standards do not prescribe a reservation database or workflow.

## Source facts and inventory consequences

- **Inventory the physical plant.** FOA's cable-plant standard calls for route, segment lengths, splice locations, component specifications, loss results and OTDR traces in documentation. Maintain unique site, closure, cable, fiber, tray and port identifiers plus location, measured length, fiber type, installation and test evidence. A route span is a geographical section; it need not be a separate cable or a separate fiber. [FOA-6 cable plants](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/1pstandards/FOA-6.pdf).
- **Number from the actual cable schedule.** One Corning 96-fiber cable has eight active tubes of 12 fibers. If sequential numbering is confirmed for that cable, tube 1 is 01–12 and tube 8 is 85–96. Store actual tube/fiber markings and the manufacturer's sequence; do not infer every cable's construction or global color convention from this example. [Corning 096EAE-T4B01A20](https://ecatalog.corning.com/optical-communications/CALA/en/Fiber-Optic-Cables/Outdoor/Outdoor-Aerial-Cables/SOLO%C2%AE-ADSS-Loose-Tube%2C-Gel-Filled%2C-Dual-Jacket-Cable/p/096EAE-T4B01A20); [FOA installation standard, color identification](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/1pstandards/FOA%20Installation%20Standard%202025%20V1.pdf).
- **Model continuity explicitly.** An uncut fiber passing a closure is continuous; a splice joins two specific fiber ends; a connector termination ends at a specific port and requires an actual patch or other connection to continue. Record each end, connection type, closure/tray or port, and as-built evidence. Fiber 01 in adjacent cables is not automatically connected to fiber 01. [ITU-T L.400, optical fibre splices](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-L.400); [FOA-6](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/1pstandards/FOA-6.pdf).
- **A route is not an optical path.** Trace ordered physical fiber segments and explicit connections from endpoint to endpoint; include patch cords, pigtails and splitter ports where present. A geographically continuous route, a common cable number, or spare capacity at each location does not by itself establish an end-to-end path. This is an inventory design inference from the documented physical plant. [FOA-6](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/1pstandards/FOA-6.pdf).
- **Splice compatibility and loss matter.** ITU-T G.652 distinguishes fiber, cable and link attributes, notes care when fusion splicing different materials, and treats splice and connector losses as link contributions. Record fiber categories and splice type, measured loss, date and test method; evaluate compatibility and acceptance against the actual installation specifications. Do not treat a splice machine estimate as proof of end-to-end acceptance. [ITU-T G.652 (2024), §§6.7 and I.1](https://www.itu.int/epublications/publication/itu-t-g-652-2024-08-characteristics-of-a-single-mode-optical-fibre-and-cable).

## PON and optical constraints

ITU defines a simple optical distribution network as a passive point-to-multipoint tree containing fibers and potentially splitters, combiners and filters. G.984.2 permits upstream and downstream transmission on the same fiber and components or on separate ones. A 1:N splitter is a physical device with one common and N branch ports, not N independent feeder fibers. [ITU-T G.9807.1 amendment, definitions 3.1.24–25](https://www.itu.int/epublications/publication/itu-t-g-9807-1-2023-amd-1-2025-05); [ITU-T G.984.2 (2019), §5](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-G.984.2-201908-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items).

Represent each splitter and its ports, the actual common-side connection, branch-side connections, and any cascaded stages. One PON group may share an upstream feeder and splitter common port while distinct services consume distinct branch ports and downstream physical paths. Splitter port availability alone does not establish a usable route. Model protection, WDM coexistence, and 2:N devices explicitly when present; do not assume every physical network is a single 1:N tree. ITU-T L.250 discusses distributed split architectures and requires design against the relevant system performance specifications. [ITU-T L.250 (2024)](https://www.itu.int/epublications/publication/itu-t-l-250-2024-01-topologies-for-optical-access-network); [ITU-T G.9805 coexistence methods](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-t/rec/g/T-REC-G.9805-202202-I%21%21SUM-HTM-E.htm).

For each OLT-to-ONU branch and each relevant wavelength/direction, calculate fiber attenuation + splice losses + connector losses + actual splitter insertion losses + engineering margin, and compare the result with the applicable optics and PON class, including receiver overload/minimum-loss limits and return-loss requirements. Avoid double counting connectors already included in a device specification. For illustration, Corning specifies up to 16.7 dB insertion loss for its particular 1×32 module; this is not a universal loss for every 1×32 splitter. G.652 Appendix I also calls for margin for modifications and aging. [Corning WMC4CC6CA6C11132](https://ecatalog.corning.com/optical-communications/US/en/Fiber-Optic-Hardware/Splitters/LS-Series-LCP-Cabinet-Splitter-Modules/p/WMC4CC6CA6C11132); [ITU-T G.652 Appendix I](https://www.itu.int/epublications/publication/itu-t-g-652-2024-08-characteristics-of-a-single-mode-optical-fibre-and-cable); [ITU-T G.984.2](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-G.984.2/en).

Illustrative arithmetic only: 10 km × 0.35 dB/km + 4 splices × 0.1 dB + 2 connector pairs × 0.3 dB + 16.7 dB splitter + 3 dB margin = **24.2 dB**. These assumed losses are not an acceptance specification; use measured lengths, wavelength-specific values and applicable equipment limits. This arithmetic alone does not prove service viability.

## Proposed reservation and consistency rules

These are operator design policies inferred from physical constraints, not requirements claimed to be stated by ITU or FOA:

1. Reserve an identified end-to-end trace, not merely a count. Track owner, purpose, status (available, reserved, assigned, out of service), start/expiry, and evidence; distinguish planned from verified physical connectivity. Release or expire a hold explicitly, retaining history.
2. Prevent conflicting simultaneous exclusive holds on the same physical fiber segment or occupied port. Validate and commit all resources for a path atomically; a failed booking leaves no partial hold. Express continuity across two spans of the same cable does not create two independent fibers. If partial-cable allocation is supported, enforce conflicts on the actual overlapping interval and its end connectivity.
3. A shared PON feeder is allocated to the PON group once. Multiple authorized branches may reference that allocation; they must not each consume another feeder core. Each branch requires an available splitter output and its own usable downstream resources. An unrelated dedicated circuit cannot use that already allocated feeder absent an explicitly engineered sharing arrangement.
4. Reject impossible joins: a fiber end or passive port cannot have two direct physical mates at the same time; distinct splitter outputs are connected through the splitter rather than spliced together. A panel termination is not an automatic through connection. Preserve valid explicit exceptions such as WDM combiners and protection devices by modeling their ports and connections.
5. Show separately physical total, unavailable/faulty, assigned, reserved and unassigned capacity per span; ensure categories are mutually exclusive for the chosen accounting scope. Count a shared feeder once per physical segment, not once per subscriber. End-to-end bookable capacity is constrained by actual reachable continuity and equipment ports, not the minimum of raw spare counts alone.
6. Before activating a proposed path, verify the as-built connections, compatibility, optical budget and applicable test results; a reservation is administrative and does not establish physical construction or performance.

## Worked example: A — T1 — T2 — T3 — B

Assume four consecutive route spans each have 96 fibers. A–T1 and T1–T2 are spans of the **same** cable X, with X:01 and X:02 uncut through T1. T2–T3 is cable Y; T3–B is cable Z. All other fibers start unassigned. T1, T2 and T3 are intermediate termination/closure locations; the stated connections are assumptions, not implied by their presence.

| Location | Dedicated P1 | PON group G1, branch to B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Panel port → X:01 | OLT port → X:02 |
| T1 | X:01 passes uncut | X:02 passes uncut |
| T2 | Splice X:01 → Y:17 | X:02 → S1 common; S1 output 3 → Y:18 |
| T3 | Splice Y:17 → Z:17 | Splice Y:18 → Z:29 |
| B | Z:17 → panel port | Z:29 → ONU |

P1 consumes X:01, Y:17 and Z:17; numbering need not match across splices. G1 consumes X:02 once as shared feeder, splitter S1 output 3, Y:18 and Z:29. With S1 assumed to be 1×8, outputs 1–2 and 4–8 remain available; their availability does not guarantee a downstream route. A second subscriber on output 4 can share X:02 only as part of G1 and needs separately traced and reserved downstream resources.

Each of the four spans has 94 locally unassigned fibers under these assumptions. X:01 is one physical fiber traversing two spans, not two independent end-to-end allocations. If Y is later fully assigned or reserved, no new dedicated A–B circuit using Y can be booked even if X and Z have spare fibers. Conversely, 94 spare per span does not prove 94 additional end-to-end paths: the necessary splices or port connections may not exist. If T1 instead terminates fibers on two panels, add the exact panel-port and patch connection before claiming continuity.

## Evidence and open decisions

Keep measured loss and OTDR results with test wavelengths, direction, reference method, date and identifiers. FOA-6 calls for connector-to-connector loss and OTDR records; OTDR diagnosis through PON splitters depends on equipment and test setup, so results must be interpreted accordingly. [FOA-6](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/1pstandards/FOA-6.pdf); [EXFO, OTDR PON testing](https://www.exfo.com/contentassets/d407e533bf304cf8a8d635f389a1bc3a/exfo_anote201_otdr-pon-testing_en.pdf).

Operator decisions still needed: whether a hold requires pre-existing splices or may include planned work; whether partial cable intervals are reservable; hold expiry and priority; treatment of faults, restoration and protection; approved fiber/connector compatibility; and the actual PON optics, loss limits and acceptance tests. Confirm installed cable construction, field labels and as-built topology before applying the example.
